01 / Current research

Lingering Spaces as a Medium of Care

Cambridge MArch Dissertation · Ongoing · Supervised by Matteo Zallio

Ongoing research · Inclusive school environments · Cross-context fieldwork

Everyday spaces for pause, encounter and emotional support

How might entrances, corridors and small outdoor areas support students' everyday experiences in high-pressure Chinese secondary schools?

Drawing on UK inclusive-school precedents and fieldwork in Zhengzhou, this research examines how spatial configuration and sensory conditions interact with lingering, informal social behaviour and emotional experience. It uses observations, questionnaires and interviews to examine how spaces are actually inhabited and adapted.

  • Three spatial settings: school thresholds, corridors and internal green spaces
  • Spatial evidence, user experiences and cross-context comparison
  • A developing design framework for more supportive everyday environments

04 / About & contact

A continuous interest in environments of care.

I am an MArch student at the University of Cambridge and a researcher with Cambridge Inclusive Design Futures (CIDF). My work explores the relationship between built environments, human experience and emotional well-being, drawing on inclusive design and evidence-based research.

My earlier research explored therapeutic environments and inclusive public spaces. At Cambridge, I investigate how the spatial and sensory conditions of informal spaces in high-pressure Chinese schools relate to students’ everyday behaviour and emotional experiences, drawing on UK precedents and fieldwork in Zhengzhou.

I am developing a PhD research proposal on inclusive ageing in China, exploring how domestic spatial conditions and AI-enabled interactions, including assistive robots, might support older adults’ autonomy, emotional comfort and sense of home.
